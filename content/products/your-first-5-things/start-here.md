## What AI and ChatGPT are

AI, in this workbook, means software that can read what you type and write a reply.

ChatGPT is OpenAI’s chat tool for that. You type a question. It writes back. You can ask it to rewrite a message so the note sounds clearer. That is the first example in this chapter.

You do not need a special vocabulary to begin. The text you paste is the example text. People also call that text a prompt. You can ignore the word.

## How to open ChatGPT

Use a phone browser if you want the shortest path. The app is optional.

### In a browser

1. Open the browser already on your phone. On an iPhone, that is often Safari. On an Android phone, that is often Chrome.
2. Go to [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com/).
3. If you see a sign-up or log-in screen, create an account or sign in.

OpenAI’s free-plan page says the free plan is available to anyone who signs up for an account. OpenAI’s help center says ChatGPT is free to use. Some tools have separate limits, and those limits change. This chapter uses ordinary text chat.

This page does not claim that signup skips a credit card. OpenAI’s pages checked for this draft do not say that.

### The official iPhone app

1. Open this App Store link: [ChatGPT for iPhone and iPad](https://apps.apple.com/app/openai-chatgpt/id6448311069).
2. Before you download, check that the developer is **OpenAI OpCo, LLC**.
3. If you search instead, search for “OpenAI ChatGPT” and check that same developer name.

OpenAI’s help center says to confirm the developer, and to use the App Store listing for the current phone and iOS requirements. Do not rely on a saved version number.

### The official Android app

1. Open this Google Play link: [ChatGPT for Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.openai.chatgpt).
2. Before you download, check that the publisher is **OpenAI**.
3. If you search instead, search for “openai chatgpt” and check that the publisher is OpenAI.

OpenAI’s help center says the same check, and says to use the Play listing for the current device requirements.

If Android sign-in fails, OpenAI’s app FAQ says Chrome and Brave are options to try, and points to its sign-in troubleshooting article.

## How to ask the first question

1. Open ChatGPT in the browser, or in the official app.
2. Sign up or sign in if asked.
3. Tap the message box. It is the place where your words go, usually at the bottom of the screen.
4. Type a question, or paste the example text below.
5. Send it. On a phone, tap the send button beside the message box.
6. Read the reply. A clear reply can still be wrong. Check anything important before you act on it or send it to someone else.
7. To ask for a change, stay in the same chat and send another message. For example: “Make that shorter.”

### How to paste on a phone

1. Copy the example text first.
2. Switch to ChatGPT.
3. Tap the message box.
4. Touch and hold in the box until **Paste** appears, then tap Paste.
5. Tap send.

If Paste does not appear, tap the message box once so the cursor is in it, then touch and hold again.

## What to leave out

Leave out passwords, account numbers, and private medical or financial details.

Read the reply before you send it to anyone. If the answer matters, check it with a person or a source you already trust.

## Your first task

Do this once before the five exercises.

You borrowed a neighbor’s ladder. You cannot return it on Saturday, because family is visiting. Sunday afternoon works. The note you typed sounds abrupt.

The note you typed:

> Hey Pat — can't bring the ladder Saturday. Something came up. Maybe next week.

An illustrative reply, written for the landing page. A live reply will use different words:

> Hi Pat — I still have your ladder. I can't return it Saturday because family is visiting. I can bring it by Sunday afternoon. Does that time work for you?

Copy this example text, paste it into a new chat, and send it:

```
Rewrite this message so it is clear, polite, and short.

I am writing to my neighbor, Pat. I borrowed a ladder and cannot return it on Saturday because family is visiting. I can bring it Sunday afternoon.

Keep my facts. Do not add new offers or extra apologies. Give me one version under 60 words.

My draft:
Hey Pat — can't bring the ladder Saturday. Something came up. Maybe next week.
```

Read the reply. If you want one more try, paste this into the same chat:

```
Shorten that to two sentences. Keep the Sunday afternoon offer, and make the tone a little warmer.
```

Stop when you have a version you would actually send. You do not have to send it to Pat.

## What comes next

These five tasks are the exercise list. Each line is the finished job. The full write-up for each one is not in this chapter yet.

1. A message you would actually send. Rewrite a tricky note to a neighbor, a friend, or a coworker.
2. A messy list, sorted. Turn a pile of tasks into what to do first tomorrow.
3. A confusing notice, in plain language. See what it says, and the one question to ask a person.
4. Questions ready for an appointment. Walk in with a short list instead of trying to remember.
5. Dinner from food you already have. Plan a meal without a new grocery trip.
